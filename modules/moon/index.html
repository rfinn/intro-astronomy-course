Welcome to the Moon

More than fifty years after Apollo astronauts last walked on the Moon, NASA is preparing to send humans back. Why return to a place that humans have already explored?

In this module, you will investigate what previous missions taught us about the Moon, what important questions remain unanswered, and why the lunar south pole has become a major target for exploration.

You will use evidence from lunar images, maps, mission results, and scientific data to evaluate possible goals and locations for future Artemis missions.

 

What Will You Investigate?

This module is organized around a series of questions:

  • Why does the Moon look and behave the way it does?
  • What did the Apollo missions teach us about the Moon?
  • How did the Moon form?
  • How do we know that water ice may exist near the lunar poles?
  • Why is NASA targeting the lunar south pole?
  • What should the first Artemis science base prioritize?

 

Your Role

Throughout this module, you will work as a member of an Artemis science-planning team.

Your Mini Inquiries will help you gather evidence about the Moon, its history, its environment, and the goals of future exploration.

At the end of the module, you will use this evidence in the Mission Artemis Investigation. You will recommend a location for a future lunar base and identify the scientific goals that the mission should prioritize.

 

By the End of This Module

You should be able to:

  • Explain the basic motions and observable behavior of the Moon.
  • Describe evidence concerning the Moon's formation and geological history.
  • Explain why water ice may survive near the lunar poles.
  • Describe the scientific, technological, and exploration goals of Artemis.
  • Compare possible lunar landing regions using scientific and engineering evidence.
  • Make an evidence-based recommendation that acknowledges competing priorities and tradeoffs.

 

How to Complete This Module

  1. Begin with the first page and work through the module in order.
  2. Complete each short knowledge check and Mini Inquiry.
  3. Keep track of the evidence you gather in each activity.
  4. Use that evidence to complete the Mission Artemis Investigation.
  5. Submit all required work through Canvas by the listed deadlines.

 

Before You Begin

No previous knowledge of astronomy is required.

During this module, you will interpret images, maps, simple graphs, and short data tables. You may also use basic ideas involving scale, motion, sunlight, and temperature.

The goal is not to memorize every fact about the Moon. The goal is to use evidence to understand why humans are returning and what future lunar exploration should accomplish.
